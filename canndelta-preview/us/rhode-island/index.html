Rhode Island Is Open. The Clock Is Running.

Rhode Island has reopened its adult-use retail licensing round, and applications are due November 23, 2026. For the first time, you do not need to be a Rhode Island resident to own a majority of a Rhode Island cannabis license.

That opens one of the Northeast’s most tightly controlled markets to operators from New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts, Connecticut and beyond. Adult-use sales are currently handled by 9 existing compassion centers. The new round is expected to add up to 24 retail licenses.

03

What Changed in 2026

  • Late 2025: About 97 applications were filed for up to 24 retail licenses.
  • April 8, 2026: A federal judge blocked the state’s residency requirement, freezing the round before the lottery.
  • June 10, 2026: Governor Dan McKee signed House Bill 8544, removing the residency requirement and tightening social equity eligibility.
  • August 7, 2026: The Cannabis Control Commission reopened applications.
  • August 11, 2026: The court injunction was lifted. The earlier round was voided and restarted.
  • November 23, 2026: Applications close.

Applied in the earlier round? You can request a refund of the $7,500 state application fee you paid to the Cannabis Control Commission. You will need to apply again to be considered.

04

What It Takes to Win a License

Licenses are awarded by lottery among qualified applicants, with no merit scoring. That means one thing matters most: a complete, compliant application submitted before the deadline.

  • Secure your location first. In the earlier round, applicants needed final municipal zoning approval before the lottery, and a similar requirement is expected this time. With only weeks left, property and zoning are the hardest part.
  • Choose the right license. Social equity certification closed in September, so most new applicants will apply for a general retail license, or a worker cooperative license if they qualify.
  • Pick an eligible town. Six municipalities have opted out of adult-use retail: Barrington, East Greenwich, Jamestown, Little Compton, Scituate and Smithfield.

Rhode Island Retail License Options

In the prior round, the 24 retail licenses were split across six zones, with no more than 4 per zone.
License Licenses (Prior Round) Application Fee (Prior Round) Annual Fee (Prior Round) Who It’s For
General Adult-Use Retailer Up to 12 (2 per zone) $7,500 $30,000 Any qualified applicant, including out-of-state owners
Worker-Owned Cooperative Retailer Up to 6 (1 per zone) $7,500 $30,000 Businesses collectively owned and controlled by their workers
Social Equity Retailer Up to 6 (1 per zone) Waived Waived in Year 1 Certified applicants only; certification closed September 11, 2026

The Commission has publicly confirmed the fees are the same as last round Talk to our team before you budget.

Not open to new applicants in this round:

  • Cannabis Cultivator: Moratorium on new licenses.
  • Compassion Center / Hybrid Retailer: Limited to existing facilities.
  • Cannabis Product Manufacturer and Independent Testing Laboratory: Contact us to discuss these paths.

Rhode Island's Six Retail Zones

Every retail application must name a location in one of these zones. Towns that have opted out of retail sales are excluded.
  • Zone 1: Burrillville, Cumberland, Glocester, North Smithfield, Smithfield, Woonsocket
  • Zone 2: Central Falls, Johnston, Lincoln, North Providence, Providence
  • Zone 3: Coventry, Foster, West Greenwich, West Warwick, Scituate
  • Zone 4: Cranston, North Kingstown, Warwick, East Greenwich
  • Zone 5: Charlestown, Exeter, Hopkinton, Narragansett, Richmond, South Kingstown, Westerly
  • Zone 6: Bristol, East Providence, Middletown, Newport, New Shoreham, Pawtucket, Portsmouth, Tiverton, Warren, Barrington, Jamestown, Little Compton
