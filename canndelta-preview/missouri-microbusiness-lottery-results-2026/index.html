Missouri’s Final Microbusiness Lottery Results Are In: 888 Applicants, 77 Licenses, and What Happens Next

Introduction

The Missouri microbusiness lottery results 2026 are officially in, marking the beginning of the final scheduled round of the state’s cannabis microbusiness licensing program.

On September 9, 2026, Missouri’s Division of Cannabis Regulation (DCR) posted the results of the state’s third microbusiness lottery following an application period that ran from July 13 through July 27. A total of 888 applications were submitted for 77 available licenses.

Round 3 represents an important milestone for a program created as part of Missouri’s 2022 constitutional amendment legalizing adult-use cannabis. The amendment requires the state to award a minimum of 144 microbusiness licenses through three separate rounds, with the licenses intended to provide a pathway into Missouri’s regulated cannabis industry for eligible individuals who might otherwise face barriers to facility ownership.

But being selected in the lottery does not mean an applicant has received a license.

DCR is now processing the top-drawn applications and conducting its review before licenses are issued. The department has made clear that lottery selection does not mean the information contained in an application has been determined to be complete, accurate, or approved. DCR can request additional information, and applicants must ultimately satisfy the state’s requirements before receiving a license.

That distinction is particularly important in Missouri microbusiness Round 3. New regulations effective May 30, 2026 strengthened the rules governing who truly owns and operates a microbusiness and increased scrutiny of business arrangements involving eligible majority owners.

For the applicants whose identifiers were selected on September 9, the lottery was therefore only the first hurdle. The eligibility review that follows will determine who ultimately receives one of Missouri’s final scheduled microbusiness licenses.

Key Takeaways

  • Missouri conducted its third and final scheduled microbusiness lottery on September 9, 2026, following 888 applications for 77 available licenses.
  • The third round is intended to bring Missouri to the constitutional minimum of 144 microbusiness licenses established under the state’s adult-use legalization amendment.
  • Being top-drawn in the lottery does not guarantee a license. DCR will review selected applications before determining whether they qualify for licensure.
  • New rules effective May 30, 2026 provide a more detailed standard for what it means for a microbusiness to be majority “owned and operated” by eligible individuals.
  • DCR can examine ownership, control, and supporting eligibility documentation and request additional information from selected applicants during its review.
  • Applicants who were not selected should not assume another scheduled lottery is coming. Round 3 completes the three-round rollout required by Missouri’s constitutional framework.

What Happened on September 9

Missouri’s September 9 drawing was considerably more than another cannabis licensing lottery. It was the final scheduled drawing in a process that began after Missouri voters approved adult-use cannabis legalization in November 2022.

The state’s microbusiness program was deliberately structured differently from its broader commercial cannabis licensing system. Rather than simply creating another category of marijuana business, the program was intended to create ownership opportunities for individuals meeting specific eligibility criteria established in Missouri’s Constitution.

For Missouri microbusiness Round 3, DCR accepted applications from July 13 through July 27, 2026. A total of 888 applications entered the process, competing for 77 available microbusiness licenses. The lottery drawing took place on September 9.

Importantly, DCR conducted the drawing using anonymized unique application identifiers rather than applicant or business names. That means the published lottery results identify the applications that were drawn, but do not publicly reveal the identities of the individuals or businesses behind them. Prospective operators should therefore be cautious about any reports claiming to identify particular businesses as Round 3 winners.

Round 3 also arrives against an important backdrop: Missouri’s experience with the first two microbusiness licensing rounds has demonstrated that receiving a license and actually opening a cannabis business are two very different milestones.

As of August 18, 2026, 46 licenses awarded during Rounds 1 and 2 remained active but were not yet operating. Only 21 microbusinesses statewide were actually operational: five dispensaries and 16 wholesale facilities.

Those numbers illustrate why September 9 should be viewed as the beginning of the next stage rather than the end of the licensing process. For selected applicants, there is still a substantial distance between having an application drawn and operating a licensed cannabis business.

Why Missouri Tightened the Rules for Round 3

The additional scrutiny facing Round 3 applicants did not emerge in a vacuum. It is largely a response to problems Missouri regulators encountered during the first two rounds of the microbusiness program.

DCR revoked dozens of previously issued microbusiness licenses after determining that some businesses did not comply with the program’s eligibility and ownership requirements. A recurring concern involved arrangements in which an individual who qualified for the microbusiness program appeared to hold majority ownership on paper, while another person or entity exercised substantial control over the business.

That distinction matters because Missouri’s microbusiness program was specifically created to provide cannabis business ownership opportunities to eligible individuals. The intent was not simply to place an eligible person’s name on an application while control or the economic benefits of the business rested elsewhere.

Regulators became particularly concerned about contractual arrangements between qualifying applicants and outside companies. In some cases, DCR characterized agreements encountered during its investigations as “predatory,” raising questions about whether the eligible individuals identified as majority owners were actually controlling and benefiting from the businesses they purportedly owned.

The disputes also demonstrated how complicated cannabis ownership can become. Control of a business is not necessarily determined by the percentage listed beside someone’s name on an organizational chart. Financing agreements, management contracts, intellectual property arrangements, voting rights, profit distributions, and other contractual relationships can potentially affect who exercises meaningful control over an operation.

Missouri responded by strengthening its microbusiness regulations.

New rules that took effect May 30, 2026 provide greater detail surrounding the requirement that microbusiness facilities be majority “owned and operated” by individuals who satisfy the program’s eligibility criteria. The changes give DCR a more developed framework for examining whether the ownership presented in an application reflects the actual structure and operation of the proposed business.

That makes Missouri microbusiness Round 3 different from the earlier lotteries in an important respect. The state is entering the eligibility review with the experience of having already investigated and revoked licenses over ownership issues.

For selected applicants, the lesson from those earlier rounds is straightforward: being drawn in the lottery is not enough. The underlying application and business arrangement must withstand DCR’s review.

What the Eligibility Review Actually Involves

This is where the process becomes particularly important for the 77 applications selected in the September 9 drawing.

Selected applicants looking for more information on what to do after winning a microbusiness license in Missouri should begin by understanding that lottery selection is only the start of DCR’s review process.

Before issuing licenses, DCR will conduct a Missouri DCR eligibility review to determine whether selected applicants satisfy the requirements of the microbusiness program. That review can include verification of the applicant’s eligibility, examination of ownership and control, review of supporting documentation, and consideration of whether the proposed business complies with Missouri’s strengthened “owned and operated” requirements.

In practical terms, DCR is looking beyond the fact that an application number was selected. Regulators need to determine whether the people identified as qualifying owners actually meet the applicable eligibility requirements and whether the ownership structure complies with the rules governing Missouri microbusinesses.

Documentation is therefore a critical part of the process. Information submitted in support of eligibility and ownership must be sufficiently complete for regulators to evaluate the application, and DCR can request additional information as part of its review.

The review can also examine whether the qualifying owners genuinely satisfy Missouri’s requirement that the microbusiness be majority owned and operated by eligible individuals. That makes the relationship between ownership on paper and actual control of the business particularly significant.

However, there is no single ownership structure or collection of documents that can be assumed to satisfy DCR in every case. Financing arrangements, management agreements, investor relationships, and other contractual obligations can differ substantially between applicants.

That is why selected applicants should treat the eligibility review as a substantive regulatory stage rather than a paperwork formality.

DCR’s experience during the first two rounds provides important context. Licenses were revoked after regulators concluded that certain applicants failed to satisfy the program’s ownership and eligibility requirements. Round 3 applicants are entering a system that has now been specifically strengthened in response to those earlier problems.

For selected applicants, the objective is therefore not simply to demonstrate that an eligible individual owns the required percentage of the company. The application must accurately reflect a business that complies with Missouri’s current microbusiness rules.

This is also an area where generalized advice has limitations. Two applications can have very different ownership arrangements, financing structures, agreements, and eligibility documentation. A potential issue for one selected applicant may have no relevance whatsoever to another.

That makes reviewing the specific application and its supporting structure particularly important before DCR completes its assessment.

Quick Reference: Round 3 By the Numbers

Missouri’s third microbusiness round can be understood particularly clearly by looking at the numbers behind the program.

The September lottery attracted hundreds of applicants, but it also comes at a time when many licenses awarded during the first two rounds have yet to become operational businesses.

The contrast between the final two numbers is particularly revealing. Missouri has already awarded dozens of microbusiness licenses through the first two rounds, but only 21 were operational as of August 18.

That does not necessarily indicate that the remaining businesses will not open. It does demonstrate, however, that winning a license and successfully launching a regulated cannabis business are separate challenges.

For Round 3 applicants, September 9 was therefore one milestone in what can still be a lengthy process. Eligibility review comes first. For those that ultimately receive licenses, the work of turning those licenses into operational businesses follows.

What Happens if You Weren't Selected

For the majority of the 888 applicants who entered Missouri’s third microbusiness lottery, September 9 brought a different result: their application identifier was not among those selected.

Those applicants have an important financial option available to them. Applicants who were not selected in the lottery can request a refund of their application fee.

The refund, however, is not immediate.

Missouri’s process requires unsuccessful applicants to wait until the Round 3 licensing process has progressed further. The application fee refund request form will not become available until 30 days after all Round 3 microbusiness licenses have been issued.

That distinction is worth noting for applicants expecting their application fee to be automatically returned following the lottery. A refund must be requested through the state’s process once the appropriate form becomes available.

Unsuccessful applicants should also be cautious about assuming there will simply be another opportunity to enter a fourth scheduled lottery.

Round 3 is the final lottery required under the original microbusiness rollout established by Missouri’s 2022 constitutional amendment. The program was structured around three application and lottery rounds designed to result in at least 144 microbusiness licenses statewide.

That means there is currently no scheduled Missouri microbusiness Round 4 waiting behind the September drawing.

Future opportunities are still possible, but they depend on what happens within the program. If licenses are revoked or otherwise become available, additional licensing opportunities could potentially arise. Applicants should therefore distinguish between the possibility of future licenses becoming available and the existence of another scheduled lottery round.

For people who remain interested in entering Missouri’s cannabis industry, the end of the scheduled microbusiness lottery process also does not necessarily mean the end of every possible route into the market.

Understanding how to get a marijuana license in Missouri requires looking at the type of cannabis business an operator wants to establish, the licenses and opportunities that are actually available, and the regulatory requirements attached to them. Missouri’s microbusiness program is only one component of the state’s broader regulated cannabis industry.

The experience of the first three microbusiness rounds also provides a useful lesson for anyone considering future licensing opportunities. Getting an application into a lottery is only one part of building a viable cannabis business. Eligibility, ownership, financing, regulatory compliance, and the ability to turn a license into an operating facility all matter after an application is selected.

For applicants who were drawn on September 9, those issues are immediate.

For applicants who were not selected, they are still worth understanding if another opportunity to participate in Missouri’s regulated cannabis market emerges.

Either way, the conclusion of Round 3 represents a significant transition for Missouri’s microbusiness program. The state has completed the three scheduled lottery rounds envisioned when voters approved the program. Attention now shifts from drawing application numbers to determining which selected applicants satisfy the eligibility requirements and, ultimately, which of those businesses successfully make the transition from a license on paper to an operating cannabis facility.
