Minnesota cannabis laws, plainly

The Minnesota basics, the way we explain them at the counter in Bayport. Who can buy, how much, where you can use it, and where you can't. General information, not legal advice.

OCM's adult-use pageWisconsin shoppers

21 or older, with a valid ID.

Adult-use cannabis is for adults 21 and older. At My Dealer we check ID every visit, no exceptions, and under 21 means not in the store at all, even with a parent.

Under Minnesota law, a valid driver's license or ID card from Minnesota, another state, or a Canadian province works, as long as it has your photo and date of birth. A U.S. passport, a valid Tribal ID, or a foreign passport also works (Minn. Stat. 342.27, subd. 4). No ID, no sale. Wisconsin residents can buy too, within Minnesota's limits. The IDs we accept.

We're an adult-use store, so we don't sell medical cannabis. Where Minnesota's medical program lives.

How much you can buy, and carry.

Minnesota caps what a retailer can sell you in one purchase (Minn. Stat. 342.27, subd. 2(c)). The same amounts are what an adult can carry in public (Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 1(a)).

TypeOne purchaseCarry in public
Flower2 ounces (pre-rolls count by weight)2 ounces
Concentrate8 grams (vapes count here)8 grams
Edible THC800 mg, shared across cannabis edibles, hemp edibles, and hemp THC drinks800 mg

At home, the flower limit is 2 pounds.

Each edible serving can have up to 10 mg THC and each package up to 200 mg. Drinks top out at 10 mg per serving and two servings per container.

Where you can use it.

Minnesota is specific about where adults can use it (Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 1(a)(7) and 1(b)). Adults 21 and older can use it:

  • In a private home, including the yard
  • On private property that isn't open to the public, unless the owner says no
  • At a business or event licensed for on-site use

You can't use it:

  • In any motor vehicle, parked or moving
  • Smoking or vaping anywhere Minnesota's Clean Indoor Air Act bans smoking
  • Smoking or vaping inside an apartment or other multifamily building, including balconies and patios
  • At public schools, state prisons, on federal property (like national parks), or in federally subsidized housing
  • Anywhere a minor would breathe the smoke or vapor
  • In public places

Staying with friends or renting a place? Ask first. Plenty of rentals and hotels ban it.

Bayport adds its own rules.

Cities can add their own rules. In Bayport, smoking or vaping cannabis in public places is banned, and that includes our parking lot. Bayport also bans open cannabis drinks on streets, sidewalks, and parking areas. There's no place to use it at My Dealer.

The code: Bayport City Code Sec. 26-77 covers smoking cannabis or hemp products in public places, and Sec. 26-28 covers open containers of cannabis or hemp drinks (Bayport Code ch. 26). Stillwater has its own public-use ban, covered on our Stillwater page.

Not in the car. Not even parked.

Minnesota law doesn't allow anyone to use cannabis in a motor vehicle, parked or moving, driver or passenger (Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 1(b)(2)). That includes our lot.

Keep it out of reach on the way home. OCM says products should ride in the trunk or rear cargo area unless they're still sealed in the original, labeled package.

Driving while impaired by cannabis is illegal in Minnesota and carries DWI penalties, even if you feel fine. Cannabis slows reaction time and judgment, and edibles can last 6 to 12 hours. Get a ride or wait it out.

Growing your own.

Yes, if you're 21 or older (Minn. Stat. 342.09, subd. 2).

Plants
Up to eight per residence, with no more than four of them mature, flowering plants at one time.
Where
At the primary residence of someone 21 or older, in an enclosed, locked space the public can't see.
Whose limit
Per home, not per person.
Concentrates
Making them at home with butane or other volatile solvents isn't allowed.

We carry cannabis seeds, so ask what's in stock.

Taxes show on the receipt.

Adult-use cannabis products carry Minnesota's 15% cannabis gross receipts tax, plus the 6.875% state sales tax and any local sales tax where you buy. Every tax shows on your receipt.

Hemp vs. cannabis is a THC line.

Botanically they're the same plant, Cannabis sativa. Legally, it comes down to THC. Under Minnesota law, a growing hemp plant has no more than 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight, and above that it's cannabis (Minn. Stat. 342.01).

Federal law uses the same 0.3% delta-9 line today. On December 11, 2026, it switches to 0.3% total THC, which counts THCA too (7 U.S.C. 1639o, the federal hemp definition). Finished hemp products with more than 0.4 mg of total THC per package, and hemp products made with lab-converted cannabinoids, also fall outside the federal definition from that date.

OCM says the federal change doesn't change how Minnesota defines lower-potency hemp edibles and drinks, or whether they're legal under Minnesota law (OCM, Federal hemp changes). Congress could still move the date. As of October 22, 2026, no delay had passed.

Minnesota also allows lower-potency hemp edibles and hemp THC drinks. OCM regulates them with the same 21+ age limit, testing, and labeling rules, and they count toward the same 800 mg limit as cannabis edibles. Everything we sell is labeled as either cannabis or hemp. Hemp vs. cannabis, the longer version.

It stays in Minnesota.

Don't take it across state lines, even into another legal state. That's a federal crime. Wisconsin is right across the river, and cannabis isn't legal there.

Every package we sell carries the state-required line: "This product may be unlawful outside the state of Minnesota."
