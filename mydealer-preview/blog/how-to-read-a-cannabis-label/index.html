How to read a cannabis label

A cannabis label is dense on purpose. Start with three things: serving vs. package, total THC, and the batch. The rest falls into place.

See the live menuCannabis 101

Guides3 min readBy My Dealer, Bayport

Start with three things.

Every regulated package carries a lot of small print. You don't need all of it at once. Serving vs. package. Total THC, for flower and vapes. Batch and testing. Get those three, and the rest of the label reads easier.

Serving vs. package.

Edibles and drinks list THC per serving and for the whole package. A 100 mg package of gummies is often ten 10 mg pieces. Read both numbers. The big one is the package. The small one is the piece.

Cannabis edibles
Up to 10 mg THC per serving and 200 mg per package
Hemp edibles
Up to 5 mg THC per serving
Drinks
Up to 10 mg THC per serving, with no more than two servings per container
One purchase
Up to 800 mg THC across cannabis edibles, hemp edibles, and hemp THC drinks combined

Those caps come from Minn. Stat. 342.27 and 342.01. For multi-serving bottles, read the serving size and measure it out. If you're new, start with 2.5 to 5 mg of THC or less. More in Edibles: start low, go slow.

Total THC, for flower and vapes.

THC % is how much of the product's weight is THC. Most of the THC in raw flower is THCA, which turns into THC when it's heated. So labels and our menu show total THC, which Minnesota defines as THCA x 0.877, plus all forms of THC (mostly delta-9).

The formula
Total THC = THCA x 0.877, plus THC
The example from our FAQ
20% THCA and 0.5% delta-9 THC works out to about 18% total THC

That's the number to compare. A higher number means stronger, not better. Aroma, terpenes, and your own experience matter just as much. If you'd rather shop by smell, our terpenes guide is the place to start.

Batch and testing.

Every package has a batch number and shows it passed state testing. That batch or lot information connects the package to its production and testing records. "Lab tested" without traceable details is only a marketing phrase.

What else is on there.

  • The ingredients, plus allergen information for manufactured products like edibles.
  • The license number of whoever grew or made it.
  • The universal cannabis symbol.
  • "Keep this product out of reach of children."
  • "This product may be unlawful outside the state of Minnesota." Every package we sell carries that line.
  • Cannabis or hemp. Everything we sell is labeled as one or the other.

Packaging should be sealed, compliant, and not designed to appeal primarily to children. If something doesn't add up, ask a budtender.

A COA isn't the whole story.

A certificate of analysis, or COA, reports laboratory results for a batch. By itself, it does not prove that a product entered Minnesota's licensed adult-use supply chain. Lower-potency hemp products can have COAs too, and some hemp categories may use out-of-state testing with different standards.

Licensed adult-use cannabis inventory is tracked in Metrc from seed to sale and must be manufactured in Minnesota. Lower-potency hemp edibles are not tracked in Metrc and can come from out of state. Minnesota's rules require physical tracking tags for plants and transfer packages, and all finished goods in a batch share the same Metrc tag. See OCM Guidance Memo GM-2026-03 and Minnesota Rule 9810.1302.

So buy adult-use cannabis from a licensed cannabis retailer, and look for the producer or manufacturer license number, the batch number, the cannabinoid profile, the required state symbols, and testing verification. Ours is Cannabis Retailer License DIS-L24-000241, and you can verify it on OCM's licensed-business list. For where hemp fits in, read hemp vs. cannabis in Minnesota.

Still squinting? Ask us anything at the counter. We'd rather slow down than guess.
