Cannabis vapes in Bayport, MN

Our vape shelf is small and changes often. Here's what we carry, how it counts toward your limit, and where you can and can't use it.

What we carry

Right now our vapes are all-in-one (AIO) devices: the battery and the cannabis oil come together, so there's nothing to pair or refill. The menu lists the oil type (for example, distillate), the strain lineage, and the size, usually 1 g. Brands on the menu when we checked on October 17, 2026: Oma. Check the vape menu for current stock before you come in.

Looking for a specific cart or brand we don't have? Ask a budtender. The selection grows as new products come in.

Limits and where to use it

Vapes count as concentrate. Minnesota caps one purchase at 8 grams of concentrate. Minn. Stat. 342.27

Vaping follows the same rules as smoking: not in any vehicle, not in public places, not anywhere Minnesota's smoking ban applies, and not where a minor would breathe it in. At home, store devices away from heat, and keep them out of reach of kids and pets.

When people choose it

A vape heats cannabis oil into an aerosol you inhale. There's no lighter, no ash, and nothing to grind or roll. People who want the quick onset of inhaling and like taking a single small puff and stopping often choose a vape.

Timing

Vaping is inhaled, so the timing is close to flower. Minnesota's Office of Cannabis Management (OCM) puts the onset at 1 to 10 minutes, the peak at about 30 minutes, and the total at 2 to 4 hours. Health Canada notes effects can last up to 6 hours, with some lingering effects up to 24.

Dosing basics

Vape oil is much more concentrated than flower. The device on our menu on October 17, 2026 was listed at 80.2% THC, while flower ran about 15% to 29%. So a single puff can deliver more THC than a puff of flower. Start with one short, gentle puff and wait 15 to 30 minutes before deciding on more, which follows OCM's start-low advice. Health Canada suggests the most caution with products over 20% THC, which includes distillates.

How it counts toward Minnesota limits

Vapes count as concentrate. One purchase can include up to 8 grams of concentrate, so a 1 g device counts as 1 g. That's also the most concentrate you can carry. Minn. Stat. 342.27, 342.09

Storage

Keep the device in a cool spot out of direct sun, and don't leave it in a hot car. Lock it up, out of reach of kids and pets. In the car, it goes in the trunk unless it's still in its sealed package. Devices have lithium batteries, so don't throw them in the trash; take them to a battery or household hazardous waste drop-off.

Practical tips

  • Count your puffs. It's easy to lose track with a vape.
  • Use only the device as made. Don't refill it, open it, or add anything to it.
  • Charge it with the cable it came with, and stop using it if it's damaged or leaking.
  • Buy licensed, tested products. A label with the batch number and THC % tells you where it came from.

Safety

Vaping isn't risk-free. OCM notes that people who smoke or vape have a higher risk of chronic bronchitis and lung irritation. The CDC linked most cases in the 2019 vaping lung injury outbreak (EVALI) to THC vapes from informal sources, and to vitamin E acetate. Don't drive after using, and don't mix cannabis with alcohol. Vaping follows the same rules as smoking: use it only in a private home or on private property where the owner allows it. Not in a vehicle, parked or moving, not in public places, and not in our parking lot. Not anywhere Minnesota's smoking ban applies, and not where a minor would breathe it.

If someone has used too much, or a child or pet gets into cannabis, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (for pets, call your vet). In an emergency, call 911.

Questions about vapes

How fast does a vape kick in, and how long does it last?

Usually 1 to 10 minutes to start, a peak around 30 minutes, and 2 to 4 hours total, according to Minnesota OCM. Some effects can last up to 6 hours.

How many puffs should I start with?

One short puff, then wait 15 to 30 minutes. Vape oil is far more concentrated than flower, so a puff goes further than you might expect.

How do vapes count toward the Minnesota limit?

They count as concentrate. One purchase can include up to 8 grams of concentrate, so a 1 g device counts as 1 g.

Can I vape in my car or in public?

No. Minnesota law doesn't allow it in a vehicle, parked or moving, or anywhere smoking is banned. It's also banned inside apartment and other multifamily buildings, including balconies and patios. Use it in a private home or on private property where the owner allows it.

Is vaping risk-free?

No. Vaping can irritate the lungs, according to Minnesota OCM. Use only licensed, tested devices as made, and don't add anything to them.

Sources: Minnesota OCM Guide to Safer Cannabis Use, Health Canada, CDC on EVALI, NIDA.
